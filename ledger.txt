Problem "Jailbreak success prediction is expensive.
Can we use internal signals to predict which
attacks will work?"

Hypothesis "Refusal-direction magnitude predicts
success for some attack families but not
others. Where it fails, the attack either
sneaks past detection or overpowers the
refusal."

Method "Trained a linear probe for refusal direction
on Qwen 3.6. Tested 4 attack families.
Measured prefill and generation refusal
magnitudes."

Key Result "Refusal direction predicts success for
role-play and injection attacks (AUC=0.85)
but fails for adversarial suffixes
(AUC=0.52). For suffixes, the refusal
direction never activates—the attack is
stealthy."

Baseline "Random guessing AUC=0.50.

Clean-prompt refusal rate predicted 60% of
variance. Our predictor beat both."
Causal Test "Steering the refusal direction changed
success rate by 40%, confirming causality."
Limitations "Tested only one model (Qwen 3.6). Need
to test generalization to other models."
