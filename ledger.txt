Research ledger

I settled on one question: can a refusal-direction probe predict model
behavior when the prompt is under attack, and can its failures distinguish
missed harm detection from compliance despite detection?

The first pilot used Qwen3-4B and showed strong harmful/harmless
separation on a small hand-written fitting set. That observation is only
a pilot. The first labeling pass scored the first 50 generated tokens
with keywords. Qwen3 was thinking by default, so those tokens were
mostly inside `<think>`. Apparent compliance counts and the 36 ambiguous
rows from that pass describe the reasoning trace, not the final answer.
I kept the pilot notebook as an audit trail and excluded every pilot
outcome number from the submission.

Corrected run (Qwen3-4B, 60 disjoint JailbreakBench/Alpaca fitting pairs,
10 battery targets, 400 rows, local JSON judge, 50 independent labels):

- Common layer 35. Thinking-on vs thinking-off direction cosine 0.087.
- Held-out Cohen's d 6.61 ± 2.14 (ON) and 7.92 ± 2.31 (OFF).
- Harm-detection AUROC 0.94–1.00 under every family, including attack.
- Compliance AUROC on harmful rows 0.70–0.85, CIs wide, no family-wise
  collapse relative to the no-attack arm.
- Completed harmful failures: 19 overpower, 7 stealth, 10 false alarms.
- Attack success 19% thinking ON vs 8% thinking OFF.
- Independent-label kappa vs judge: 0.57 / 0.53 / 0.56. Judge vs old
  keyword labeler: 0.92 agreement, kappa 0.84.
- Differential steering (α = 5, 10, 20): stealth rescue 0 at every
  alpha; overpower rescue 0.38 / 0.25 / 0.31. Cluster difference CIs
  exclude zero in the opposite of the preregistered direction.
- Ablation on clean harmful prompts: 3/10 new compliances thinking OFF,
  0/10 thinking ON.

The OpenAI judge was abandoned after the org's 50 request/day cap on
both gpt-5-mini and gpt-4o-mini. Reported labels use Qwen3-4B as a local
JSON judge. That limitation is in the write-up.
