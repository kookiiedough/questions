But — be honest with your friend about the novelty risk. This is a fairly direct extension of "Refusal is Mediated by a Single Direction" (his own scholars' paper, cited in the doc). He's explicit that "showing a safety-related concept has a linear representation" without a twist is a common rejected pattern. If the project is just "confirm the refusal direction predicts refusal," that's not enough — it needs a genuine surprise (e.g., does this generalize across models with different post-training? does it fail in an interesting way for some attack class? can you use the prediction to make red-teaming meaningfully more efficient in practice, not just in principle?).That's legitimate defensive research, but if they write it up, they should think about how they frame it — an "automated red-teaming platform" that predicts which attacks work most efficiently is useful for building better defenses, but it's also useful for building better attacks, and that dual-use framing is worth addressing head-on in the writeup rather than ignoring.
